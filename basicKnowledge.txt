OpenTelemetry(OTel) is a vendor-neutral open source Observability framework for instrumenting, generating, collecting, and exporting telemetry data such as traces, metrics, and logs.

How Otel even founded?
OpenTracing and OpenCensus merged in 2019 to address fragmentation in the observability ecosystem. OpenTracing focused mainly on distributed tracing and provided a vendor-neutral instrumentation API, while OpenCensus offered tracing and metrics with a more implementation-oriented approach. Their overlapping goals but different architectures and lack of mutual compatibility created confusion for developers, duplicated effort, and made standardization difficult. OpenTelemetry unified their strengths into a single, vendor-neutral project, simplifying instrumentation and improving interoperability across observability tools.
==OpenTracing focused on providing a vendor-neutral API for distributed tracing.
==OpenCensus supported both tracing and metrics, including telemetry collection and export.

Difference between Observability and Monitoring?
Observe: 
- Observability lets you understand a system from the outside by letting you ask questions about that system without knowing its inner workings
- It also helps you answer the question “Why is this happening?” [*]
- Observability is a fashionable word with some admirably nerdy and academic origins. In control theory, “observability” measures how well we can understand the internals of a given system using only its external outputs. 
Monitoring:
-- Monitoring is the process of collecting data and generating reports on different metrics that define system health.
[*] : To ask questions, application must be properly instrumented. That is, the application code must emit signals such as traces, metrics, and logs.
Signals: Signals are Infra/app/OS outputs that describe the underlying activity, logs, metrics, traces, etc. they're all kind of a signal.
Trace: The path of a request through your application. Traces give us the big picture of what happens when a request is made to an application.
Metric: A metric is a measurement of a service captured at runtime. 
logs: A log is a timestamped text record, either structured (recommended) or unstructured, with optional metadata. Of all telemetry signals, logs have the biggest legacy.
